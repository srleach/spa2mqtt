    Hardware and wiring¶

    This page lists the parts you need and shows how to connect the ESP32 to the tub.

    Mains voltage

    A spa contains mains voltage and can deliver a large fault current. Turn off the spa at its breaker before you open the equipment bay or touch the control board. Check that the board is dead before you work on it. If you are not competent to work near mains wiring, get an electrician to do it. Read Safety first.

    What you need¶

    Part Why you need it
    An ESP32 board Runs ESPHome and the spa_bus component.
    An RS485 transceiver Converts the bus's differential signal to the 3.3 V serial signal of the ESP32. A differential signal is carried as the voltage difference between two wires, A and B.
    Twisted-pair cable Carries A and B. Twisting the pair rejects electrical noise from pumps and heaters.
    A DC power supply for the ESP32 Powers the ESP32 independently of the tub. See Power.
    A connector that fits the tub's RS485 header Lets you unplug the ESP32 without cutting wires.

    You can use one of two hardware options.

    The project's example device uses this board. It has an ESP32-S3, an onboard RS485 transceiver, Ethernet, 8 relays and 8 digital inputs. The RS485 transceiver connects to GPIO17 (TX) and GPIO18 (RX).

    The example YAML sets no flow_control_pin, because the onboard transceiver switches between send and receive by itself. Check the board's schematic if you use a different revision.

    The ready-made device file is esphome/example-jacuzzibridge-esp.yaml.

    Any ESP32 with a free UART works. Add a 3.3 V RS485 transceiver module, for example one based on a MAX3485 or SP3485 chip. Do not use a 5 V MAX485 module directly: its receive output can put 5 V on an ESP32 pin, and ESP32 pins tolerate 3.3 V only.

    • Connect the module's RO (receiver output) to the ESP32's RX pin.
    • Connect the module's DI (driver input) to the ESP32's TX pin.
    • Many modules have DE (driver enable) and RE (receiver enable) pins. DE turns on the transmitter. RE turns on the receiver. Tie them together and connect them to a spare GPIO, then set that GPIO as flow_control_pin. The component then turns the transmitter on only while it sends. Some modules switch direction automatically and have no DE/RE pins. Those need no flow_control_pin.

    The tub's RS485 header¶

    The J235 control board has an RS485 header for a second topside panel. A topside panel is the control panel on the rim of the tub. Most tubs have only one panel, so the second-panel header is usually free. It is the intended place to attach a monitoring device.

    The bus is shared. The control board, the topside panel and any add-on device sit on the same two wires. The control board decides who may talk:

    • The board broadcasts a status frame about three times per second. Every device on the bus hears it. This is all the ESP32 needs in receive-only mode.
    • The board polls each panel channel in turn with a clear-to-send frame (CTS). A channel is a bus address that the board has given to one client. A client may transmit only in the short gap right after the CTS for its own channel.
    • A new device must ask the board for a channel before it can send. The ESP32 does this only when transmit is enabled and you issue the first command.

    A receive-only ESP32 never drives the bus, so it cannot collide with the topside panel. See the wire protocol for the details.

    Wiring¶

    1. Connect header A to transceiver A (sometimes labelled A+ or D+).
    2. Connect header B to transceiver B (sometimes labelled B- or D-).
    3. Connect header 0 V to the transceiver's ground. RS485 tolerates some ground difference, but a common ground keeps the signal within the transceiver's range and makes reception reliable.
    4. Leave the header's +12 V unconnected unless you have read Power.

    A and B swapped

    Manufacturers do not agree on which wire is A and which is B. If the ESP32 logs no status frames and the UART settings are correct, swap A and B. A swap does no damage.

    Termination

    RS485 buses use a 120 ohm resistor at each end to stop signal reflections. The tub's own wiring is short and already works with its panel. Leave the transceiver's termination resistor or jumper off unless reception is unreliable.

    Power¶

    The tub's header carries 12 V. The legacy wiring notes for the J235 list it as "12v +/, A/B": a 12 V supply pair and the A/B pair. The current rating of that 12 V supply is not known. The supply also feeds the topside panel. An ESP32 with Wi-Fi can draw short peaks of several hundred milliamps. Overloading the supply could disturb the panel or the board.

    Power the ESP32 from its own supply unless you know the header's rating.

    • The Waveshare ESP32-S3-ETH-8DI-8RO takes 7-36 V DC on its power terminal, or 5 V over USB-C. Check your board's documentation for its input range before you connect any supply.
    • Other ESP32 boards usually take 5 V over USB. Check the board's documentation.

    If you decide to use the header's 12 V, use a board or a regulator that accepts 12 V, and add a fuse in the 12 V line.

    UART settings¶

    The bus runs at 115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit (115200 8N1). The tub definition sets the baud rate. ESPHome checks at compile time that your uart: block matches it. The uart: defaults for data bits, parity and stop bits are already 8N1.

    Set logger: baud_rate: 0 on boards where the logger would share the bus UART. This keeps log output off the RS485 pins. Logs still reach you over Wi-Fi through the API.

    Mounting¶

    • Mount the ESP32 in a dry enclosure, away from the heater and the pumps. The equipment bay gets hot and humid.
    • Route the low-voltage cable away from mains cables, and fix it so it cannot touch moving or hot parts.
    • Close every cover before you restore power at the breaker.
